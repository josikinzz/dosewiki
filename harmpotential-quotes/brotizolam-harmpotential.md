## Source: Wikipedia

### Side Effects

Common side effects of brotizolam are typical of hypnotic benzodiazepines and are related to CNS depression, and include **somnolence, ataxia, headache, anterograde amnesia, dizziness, fatigue, impairment of motor functions, slurred speech, confusion, and clumsiness**.

Less common side effects include **hypotension, respiratory depression, hallucinations, nausea and vomiting, palpitations, and paradoxical reactions (i.e. aggression, anxiety, violent behavior, etc.)**.

### Residual Impairment

**Brotizolam can cause residual side effects the next day such as impaired cognitive and motor functions as well as drowsiness.**

### Sleep Disruption

**Disruption of sleep patterns may also occur such as suppression of REM sleep.**

### Rebound Insomnia

**Mild rebound insomnia may occur in some patients when treatment is stopped.**

### Not Ideal Hypnotics

**Benzodiazepines and thienodiazepines are therefore not ideal hypnotics in the treatment of insomnia.**

### Elderly Concerns

**The suppression of deep sleep stages by either may be especially problematic to the elderly as they naturally spend less time in the deep sleep stage.**

### Abuse Potential

**Brotizolam is a drug with a potential for abuse.** Drug misuse is defined as taking the drug to achieve a 'high', or continuing to take the drug in the long term against medical advice.

### Abuse History

**Abuse of brotizolam, although not widespread, was a problem in Hong Kong back in the late 1980s and 1990s.** To control benzodiazepine abuse in Hong Kong, **the Government's Pharmacy and Poisons Board reclassified benzodiazepines as Dangerous Drugs in October 1990**.

### Trafficking Problems

**Particular problems arose with the trafficking and abuse of nimetazepam and the abuse of temazepam** within that same year in 1991.

### Contraindications

**Thienodiazepines and benzodiazepines require special precaution if used in the elderly, during pregnancy, in children, alcohol or drug-dependent individuals and individuals with comorbid psychiatric disorders.**
