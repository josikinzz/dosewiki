## Source: Erowid

### Basics

5-MeO-DMT is a psychoactive tryptamine which was first identified in the mid 20th Century as a chemical in a South American plant entheogen. It is a common constituent of many different plants and has been used traditionally in psychoactives snuffs such as Yopo from the Anadenanthera colubrina seeds, Epena from Virola sap, and some ayahuasca-type brews where 5-MeO-DMT containing plants are used as admixtures.

### History

In the early 1950s, 5-MeO-DMT was identified as one of the main psychoactive ingredients in 'cohoba' snuffs used in the northern Amazon basin. Its use in the form of snuffs and brews has been documented as early as the 8th Century A.D. from a burial site in Northern Chile which found snuffing paraphernalia and traces of N,N-DMT, 5-MeO-DMT and Bufotenine. Other archeological sites have also uncovered seeds of 5-MeO-DMT containing Anadenanthera species from around the same time period. Its use as a recreational psychoactive has increased through the 80's and 90's.

- **Florida:** Schedule I in Florida.
- **Louisiana:** Schedule I (as of June 2013).
- **China:** Controlled in China as a Category I psychotropic substance as of September 2010.
- **Denmark:** As of December 1, 2004, 5-MeO-DMT is legally restricted to "medical or scientific purposes".
- **Finland:** 5-MeO-DMT was banned in Finland in December 2014.
- **Germany:** Schedule I / Highest level of control, unable to be prescribed, manufactured, or possessed as of Sep, 1999.

## Source: Wikipedia

5-MeO-DMT was first synthesized in 1936, and in 1959 it was isolated as one of the psychoactive ingredients of Anadenanthera peregrina seeds used in the preparation of Yopo snuff. It was once believed to be a major component of the psychoactive effects of the snuff, although this has recently been shown to be unlikely, due to the limited or sometimes even non-existent amount of it within the seeds, which instead achieve their effects from the 5-OH-DMT (bufotenin) present in them. It is metabolized by CYP2D6.

### History

In addition to its traditional use as an entheogen by South American shamans, 5-MeO-DMT has been used in modern times as a recreational drug. It was first synthesized in 1936 by Toshio Hoshino and Kenya Shimodaira.

#### Toad venom

The first published report of toad venom as a source of 5-MeO-DMT was in 1968. In his book TiHKAL, Shulgin describes the use of the venom of the Colorado River toad (Incilius alvarius), which contains up to 15% 5-MeO-DMT.

### Legal status

As of October 2015, 5-MeO-DMT is a controlled substance in China. It is also illegal in Australia, Germany, Greece, Mexico, New Zealand, Sweden, Switzerland, Turkey, and the United Kingdom.
In the United States, it was made a Schedule I controlled substance in January 2011.
In 2024, the United States DEA announced its intent to place 5-MeO-DMT into Schedule I.
In the Netherlands, 5-MeO-DMT is not a listed drug in the Opium Law, but it is considered an analogue of bufotenin, which is a List I drug.
In Canada, 5-MeO-DMT is not specifically listed in the CDSA, but it is possible that it could be considered an analogue of DMT.
In Finland, 5-MeO-DMT is a controlled substance as of 2014.
In Russia, 5-MeO-DMT is a controlled substance as of 2011.
