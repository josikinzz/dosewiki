A case-by-case audit of DoseWiki’s unknown, glitchy, misleading, missing, and thumbnail-less replication media—traced through Effect Index, PsychonautWiki, the Internet Archive, and creator sources before any canonical correction is staged.

01

The brief

Current condition

Metadata inherited from filenames

  • Unknown often survives despite a name inside the filename.
  • Hash-like, technical, or “glitchy” labels appear as titles.
  • The same underlying image may have duplicate records.
  • Missing media and absent thumbnails break visual identification.
  • One artist field collapses replication creator and underlying artist.
Desired condition

Evidence-bearing canonical records

  • Readers see a defensible title and human-readable credit.
  • Historical captions remain available when they add context.
  • Creator roles are represented without claiming more than evidence supports.
  • Every visible asset and thumbnail resolves.
  • The gallery, lightbox, and article embed agree automatically.
“Fingerprint → trace → decide → stage → verify.”The leading words for every item. The same process repeats even when the answer differs.
In scope

Suspect image records

Unknown creators; filenames as titles; duplicated, mismatched, or weak metadata; formal artworks presented as replications.

Also in scope

Broken media surfaces

Missing originals, unavailable URLs, broken video poster frames, missing thumbnails, and records that cannot be visually reviewed.

Later branch

Known media spot checks

Expand beyond “unknown first” only after the workflow proves reliable and review throughput is understood.

Completion criterion
The suspect-set query is reproducible and accounts for every record with unknown credit, placeholder title, failed media, failed thumbnail, duplicate fingerprint, or archive disagreement.
02

Editorial rules

Purpose-made replication

Credit the maker

Use the documented replication title and the person who produced the perceptual edit, animation, photograph, or render.

Pre-existing artwork

Credit the original artist

Use the formal work title and artist when Effect Index reused an artwork as an experiential example. Preserve the historical caption separately.

Transformed source

Credit both roles

Represent “replication by X, based on work by Y” when the evidence identifies both. Do not flatten the roles into one ambiguous name.

No formal title

Write a descriptive label

Use a restrained editorial label, marked internally as editorial. A description is not presented as the creator’s formal title.

Archive-only evidence

Record exactly what it supports

A clear archived Effect Index or PsychonautWiki caption is sufficient. Store the URL, timestamp, quotation, and screenshot reference.

Unresolved identity

Keep uncertainty visible

Use “Creator unknown” or a role-qualified uncertainty note. Visual resemblance and filename implication become leads, not facts.

Evidence hierarchy, not evidence inflation. First-party creator pages strengthen a finding but are not mandatory. An archived caption can settle the public label. Search snippets, visual style, and filename parsing can only open a lead until a source explicitly connects identity to the media.

Research pipeline

02TraceJoin local Effect Index dumps; inspect archived Effect Index and PsychonautWiki pages; follow creator leads.

04StageProduce a digest-bound proposal containing exact before/after metadata and media operations.
05VerifyReview screenshots and tests across gallery, lightbox, effect article, and category article surfaces.
Automated legwork

Inventory before browsing

  • Run npm run convex:doctor before any future Convex write—not during planning.
  • Read live records through the public/query surface.
  • Resolve every original and thumbnail URL.
  • Compute byte hash and perceptual hash.
  • Normalize filename, title, and creator tokens for candidate generation.
  • Join Effect Index IDs and effect associations from local dumps.
  • Produce a browser-ready contact sheet grouped by symptom.
Computer-use legwork

Inspect what text cannot prove

  • Open the live gallery and exact article embedding surfaces.
  • Compare candidate media side by side at full resolution.
  • Navigate Wayback captures where static fetching cannot expose interactive pages.
  • Capture captions, bylines, surrounding context, and archive timestamps.
  • Verify broken-image and missing-thumbnail behavior visually.
  • Record screenshot identifiers in the audit row.
Computer Use protocol: inspect app state before each interaction sequence, use accessibility elements where possible, and re-inspect after actions. Screenshots support visual identity; archived text supports attribution.
Completion criterion
An item leaves research only when its media has been visually identified, its best historical source has been captured, and the proposed action is explicit—even when that action is “hold unresolved.”

06

Missing media & thumbnail recovery

A. Locate

Find candidate bytes

Check Convex storage fallback fields, R2 URLs, local source folders, Wayback captures, archived page resources, and original hosting IDs.

B. Authenticate

Match identity

Compare filename, dimensions, duration, first/last frames, perceptual hash, and surrounding archived caption.

C. Normalize

Prepare safe asset

Preserve an archival original; create a web-safe derivative only when required. Record every transformation and checksum.

D. Restore

Stage storage change

Upload only after review, patch the exact record, regenerate thumbnails deterministically, then verify public rendering.

Image recovery checks

  • Decode successfully and report real MIME type.
  • Strip or inspect risky metadata before serving.
  • Retain aspect ratio and adequate resolution.
  • Compare recovered image against archived screenshot.

Video thumbnail checks

  • Decode video and record duration.
  • Select a representative non-black frame deterministically.
  • Use consistent output format and dimensions.
  • Ensure poster URL resolves independently of video playback.
Rights continuity: recovering bytes does not create reuse permission. Preserve or update rights_status, source_url, rightsholder, and permission_notes based only on evidence.
Completion criterion
Every recovery proposal includes source provenance, checksums, a visual match, derivative notes, rights status, and successful local rendering of both media and thumbnail.

Preferred deep module

One audit command

A narrow CLI interface hides URL resolution, hashing, archive joins, duplicate grouping, and report generation. Its outputs—not internal helper layers—are the durable contract.

Deletion test

No pass-through “provenance service”

Keep research tooling beside replication operations. Create a runtime abstraction only when it hides genuine role-aware credit behavior used by multiple consumers.

Completion criterion
The final implementation file list is derived from the tracer bullet. Every modified runtime file has a specific behavior change and test; speculative abstractions remain out of scope.

09

Batches & human review

CANARY1–3 recordsOne title/artist correction, one missing thumbnail, optionally one missing image.
BATCH 1Explicit archive matchesUnknown records whose archived captions clearly name title and creator.
BATCH 2Filename recoveriesNames currently trapped in filenames but confirmed by archive evidence.
BATCH 3Role conflictsPre-existing art, transformed works, creator-versus-uploader distinctions.
BATCH 4Recovery & mergesMissing bytes, thumbnails, duplicates, dead-host replacements.

Reviewer packet

  • Side-by-side current and proposed display.
  • Current media plus archive/creator screenshots.
  • Brief evidence quote and clickable source URLs.
  • Role and naming-branch explanation.
  • Exact metadata and storage operations.
  • Uncertainty and rights notes.

Batch stop rule

Stop after each batch and inspect public previews. Continue only when the reviewed proposal count equals the applied count, no unrelated records changed, and every consumer surface matches the approved packet.

Publishing to Convex remains a separate, explicit action after npm run convex:doctor and human approval.

Completion criterion
Every applied record is traceable to one approved proposal digest, and every proposal is classified as applied, rejected, superseded, or still pending.

Deliberately left out

Not part of the first execution

  • Public user-submission or correction UI.
  • Automated facial/style recognition of artists.
  • Broad redesign of the replication gallery.
  • Re-licensing media without documented permission.
  • Auditing every apparently known video/audio record.
Definition of done

The unknown-first pass closes when…

  • Every suspect record has an audit disposition.
  • Every accepted credit is backed by archived evidence.
  • Every title is classified by provenance.
  • Every missing asset/thumbnail is recovered or explicitly unavailable.
  • Every approved correction renders consistently everywhere.
  • Every unresolved record includes a search log and honest public wording.
The project succeeds by making uncertainty legible—not by forcing every image to have a confident answer.Accuracy is the deliverable; lower “Unknown” counts are a consequence, not the target.
