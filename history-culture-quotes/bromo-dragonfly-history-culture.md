## Source: PsychonautWiki

Bromo-DragonFLY (also known as DOB-DragonFLY or simply B-DFLY or Dragonfly) is a novel psychedelic substance of the amphetamine and benzodifuran classes. It was first synthesized in 1998 by Matthew Parker from DOB-FLY.

Bromo-DragonFLY is extremely potent and produces unusually long effects which reportedly can last up to several days. It briefly gained popularity among research chemicals circa 2010 until several deaths occurred after the substance was accidentally mislabeled and sold as 2C-B-FLY, leading to its prohibition.

### Notable deaths

On May 7, 2011, in the United States, two young adults died and several others were hospitalized after overdosing on Bromo-DragonFLY, which they believed was 2C-E.

## Source: Wikipedia

Bromo-DragonFLY, also known as 3C-Bromo-Dragonfly or DOB-Dragonfly, is a psychedelic drug of the phenethylamine, DOx, and FLY families. Bromo-DragonFLY was first described in the scientific literature by David E. Nichols and colleagues in 1998. It was encountered as a novel designer drug in 2006.

### Overdose and toxicity

Laboratory testing has confirmed that in October 2009, a batch of Bromo-DragonFLY was distributed, mislabeled as the related compound 2C-B-FLY, which is around 20 times less potent than BDF by weight. This mistake is believed to have contributed to several lethal overdoses and additional hospitalizations.
Vasoconstrictive action resulting from severe overdose of Bromo-DragonFLY is known to have caused tissue necrosis of the extremities in at least one case. In September 2007, a 35-year-old Swedish male required amputation of the front part of his feet and several fingers on one hand after taking a massive (but unknown) overdose.

### Legal status

- **Australia:** Nationally, the drug is listed under Schedule 9 (Prohibited) of the Poisons Standard.
- **Canada:** As of Oct 12, 2016, Bromo-DragonFLY is listed in Schedule III of the Canadian Controlled Drugs and Substances Act.
- **Denmark:** On December 3, 2007, the drug was banned in Denmark.
- **Finland:** As of 12 March 2012, Bromo-DragonFLY is an illegal designer drug.
- **Sweden:** Sveriges riksdag added Bromo-Dragonfly to schedule IV as narcotics in Sweden as of Jan 3, 2008. Bromo-DragonFLY was first classified as "health hazard" by Statens folkhälsoinstitut as of Jul 15, 2007.

### Dosage

Bromo-Dragonfly is a chemical which has very little history of human use and was used as a novel brain research chemical with rats in the mid 1990s at Purdue. In May 2005, a quantity of this chemical became available in some human experimental psychoactive markets.
