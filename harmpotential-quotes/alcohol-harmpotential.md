## Source: DrugBank

### Lethal Dose

**LD50: 5628 mg/kg (mouse)**

### Overdose Effects

An overdose of ethanol may result in **CNS depression, acute respiratory failure or death**.

### Adverse Drug Reactions

- **CNS depression** - severity increased
- **Respiratory depression** - severity increased
- **Gastrointestinal irritation** - severity increased
- **Bleeding time** - severity increased
- Risk or severity of **hypertension** can be increased with multiple drug combinations

### Dangerous Interactions

The risk or severity of:
- **Bleeding** can be increased when combined with anticoagulants
- **Heart Failure** can be increased when combined with certain medications
- **Hypotension** can be increased with various drug combinations
- **Liver damage** can be increased when combined with hepatotoxic substances

## Source: Drug Users Bible

### Hard Drug Classification

Alcohol is a **hard drug**. It is also **highly addictive**, and **culturally normalised and marketed globally**.

### Death Statistics

In 2020 there were **8,697 alcohol related deaths** in the United Kingdom. In the United States there were an **estimated 95,000 deaths**.

### Binge Drinking Dangers

**Binge drinking is particularly dangerous**. Drinking spirits, for example, can quickly push BAC levels to **dangerous or even fatal levels**.

### Blood Alcohol Poisoning Risk

At 0.31-0.45 **there is significant risk of death**. Anything above this equates to **alcohol blood poisoning**.

## Source: Safer Party

### Life-Threatening Doses

**Very high doses (blood alcohol concentration of 3-4‰) can be life-threatening** due to respiratory arrest, a coma, or cardiovascular failure.

### Binge Drinking Effects

**Binge drinking puts a lot of strain on the body** and increases the risk of accidents due to the lack of overview when drunk.

### Dependence Warning

**Alcohol can cause both psychological and physical dependence.**

### Dangerous Combinations

- **Depressants (GHB/GBL, benzodiazepines, opiates, ketamine)**: Risk of **unconsciousness and respiratory depression increases massively** - combination can be **life threatening**
- **Cannabis**: The effects become **unpredictable**
- **Cocaine**: Greater **risk of alcohol poisoning** because the intoxication cannot be felt as strongly

## Source: Wikipedia

### Addiction Status

**Alcohol is addictive to humans**, and can result in **alcohol use disorder, dependence and withdrawal**.

### Reduced Lifespan

The life expectancy of someone with an alcohol use disorder is **reduced by about 12 years** compared to average.

### No Safe Amount

The World Health Organization stated "there is **no safe amount that does not affect health**".

### Organ Damage

Alcohol can cause **irreversible damage to the brain and body systems**. It damages every organ in the body, but its most dramatic impact is on the **liver**, where it can cause severe conditions including **fatty liver, hepatitis, and cirrhosis**.

### Cancer Risk

All alcoholic drinks, including red and white wine, beer, and liquor, are linked with **cancer**. The more you drink, the higher your **cancer risk**.

### Brain Damage

Long-term alcohol abuse can cause a number of physical symptoms, including **cirrhosis of the liver, pancreatitis, epilepsy, polyneuropathy, alcoholic dementia, heart disease, nutritional deficiencies, peptic ulcers** and **sexual dysfunction**, and can eventually be **fatal**.

### Withdrawal Severity

Alcohol withdrawal can cause symptoms ranging from mild effects such as insomnia, tremors and irritability to severe and **potentially fatal symptoms such as delirium tremens, seizures**, and cardiovascular collapse.

### Overdose Death

Once a BAC reaches about **0.3%–0.4%** the individual is at high risk of going into a **coma or dying** from respiratory depression and physical responses to alcohol withdrawal like vomiting (and resultant aspiration of said vomit).

### Fetal Harm

Drinking during pregnancy can cause **brain damage to the fetus**. Alcohol use during pregnancy can cause **fetal alcohol spectrum disorders (FASDs)**, a range of problems that can happen when a fetus is exposed to alcohol.

### Poisoning Fatalities

In the United States, about **2,200 alcohol poisoning deaths** are reported each year.

### Dependence Development

Alcohol dependence is linked to a **lifespan that is reduced by about 12 years relative to the average person**.
