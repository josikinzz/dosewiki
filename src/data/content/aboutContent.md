**dose.wiki** compiles knowledge about psychoactive drugs into one open dataset that anyone can read here or download. It holds four collections: **{{compoundCount}}** [substance articles](/substances), **{{effectCount}}** [subjective effect articles](/effects), **{{replicationCount}}** [replications](/replications), and **{{reportCount}}** [trip reports](/reports).

A substance article covers dose, duration, effects, interactions, chemistry, and legality by country. An effect article describes one component of an altered state, from visual drifting to ego death. A replication is an image, video, or sound that reproduces an effect, made by an artist who has experienced it. A trip report is one person's account of one experience.

Most of the substance knowledge already existed, spread across ten reference sites in ten different formats. The people behind dose.wiki compiled hundreds of substances by hand for earlier reference sites, so we know what that takes. Each substance is days of work. Each writer sets a different standard. When a writer leaves, the method leaves with them.

So substance articles start from a pipeline. Code transcribes the numbers. An LLM reads the sources and drafts each section from word-for-word excerpts, with a citation for what it took from where. Human editors take over from there. They decide what is accurate and safe, and they add what the sources left out. Each article's status subsection shows how much of that work it has received, and [How articles are made](/docs/how) shows each step and each prompt.

The effect articles and replications are people's work. The Subjective Effect Index was written by contributors over more than a decade, across several websites, and dose.wiki is its current home. Replications are made by human artists, most of them from the [r/replications](https://reddit.com/r/replications) community on Reddit, each one trying to show what an experience looked like from the inside. No pipeline drafts those.

We built it this way because the cost of the work changed. The information needed to compile a substance reference is published and available. Compiling it was always the expensive part, and compiling is now the part that these tools do well. People spend their time on the parts that need a person: judgment and art.

Everything we write is dedicated to the public domain under [CC0](/docs/license), including the articles and the schema behind the dataset. The site's code is MIT. Trip reports, replications, the TripSit interaction data, and reagent test data keep their own terms. You can download the datasets on the [Open Data](#data) tab, use them in your own project, or build another encyclopedia on top of them. We want that to happen.

dose.wiki is in **beta**. The database is live and growing, coverage is incomplete, and most substance articles are still at their baseline, waiting for human review. Structure and features will keep changing on the way to 1.0. If something looks wrong or missing, tell us through the [feedback form](/about/feedback) or at <contact@dose.wiki>.
