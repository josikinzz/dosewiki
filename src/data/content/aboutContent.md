**dose.wiki** compiles the published knowledge about psychoactive drugs into one open dataset that anyone can read here or download. Today it holds **{{compoundCount}}** substance articles, **{{effectCount}}** documented subjective effects, and **{{reportCount}}** trip reports, with molecule drawings, dose and duration tables, interaction data, and legality by country.

Most of this knowledge already existed, spread across ten reference sites in ten different formats. [Josie Kins](/contributors/josie), who co-founded dose.wiki, compiled hundreds of substances by hand for earlier reference sites. That work showed the cost of a hand-built reference: one substance takes days, the standard changes with each writer, and when a writer leaves, the way they worked leaves with them.

So each article here starts from a pipeline. Code transcribes the numbers. An LLM reads the sources and drafts each section from word-for-word excerpts, with a citation for what it took from where. Human editors take over from there. They decide what is accurate and safe, and they add what the sources left out. Each article's status subsection shows how much of that work it has received, and [How articles are made](/docs/how) shows each step and each prompt.

We built it this way because the cost of the work changed. The information needed to make a substance encyclopedia is published and available. Compiling it was always the expensive part, and compiling is now the part that these tools do well. The baseline is built once, stays consistent across articles, and can be rebuilt as the tools improve. People spend their time on judgment instead of transcription.

Everything we write is dedicated to the public domain under [CC0](/docs/license), including the articles and the schema behind the dataset. The site's code is MIT. Trip reports, the TripSit interaction data, and reagent test data keep their own terms. You can download the datasets on the [Open Data](#data) tab, use them in your own project, or build another substance encyclopedia on top of them. We want that to happen.

dose.wiki is in **beta**. The database is live and growing, coverage is incomplete, and most articles are still at their baseline, waiting for human review. Structure and features will keep changing on the way to 1.0. If something looks wrong or missing, tell us through the [feedback form](/about/feedback) or at [contact@dose.wiki](mailto:contact@dose.wiki).
