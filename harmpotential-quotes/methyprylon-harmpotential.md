## Source: DrugBank

### Withdrawal from Market Due to Adverse Events

**"Methyprylon was withdrawn from the U.S. market in June 1965 and the Canadian market in September 1990 due to adverse events."**

### Overdose Symptoms

**"Symptoms of overdose include excitation and convulsions."**

### CNS Depression Effects

**"Methyprylon depresses the activity of muscle tissues, the heart, and the respiratory system."**

### Respiratory and Cardiac Depression

**"CNS depressant effects, including mental depression and confusion, clumsiness, lack of coordination, respiratory depression, confusion, drowsiness, lethargy, swelling of feet or lower legs, dizziness"**

### Drug Interactions Increasing CNS Depression Risk

**"The risk or severity of CNS depression can be increased when Methyprylon is combined with [various drugs including 1,2-Benzodiazepine, Acetazolamide, Acetophenazine, Agomelatine, Alfentanil]."**

### Adverse Effects - General Side Effects

**"Side effects can include skin rash, fever, depression, ulcers or sores in mouth or throat, unusual bleeding or bruising, fast heartbeat, CNS depressant effects, including mental depression and confusion, clumsiness, lack of coordination, respiratory depression, confusion, drowsiness, lethargy, swelling of feet or lower legs, dizziness, headache, double vision, constipation, diarrhea, nausea and/or vomiting, ataxia, unusual weakness, clumsiness"**

### Overdose Pharmacokinetics Note

**"A case report found that the pharmacokinetics of methyprylon were not concentration dependent in an overdose case; explanations included saturation or inhibition of metabolic pathways. The generally accepted half-life for a therapeutic dose was not found appropriate in intoxicated patients and would underestimate the time required to reach a safe concentration of the drug."**
