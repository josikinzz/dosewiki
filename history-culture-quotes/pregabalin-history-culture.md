## Source: The Drug Classroom

Richard Silverman of Northwestern University asked Dr. Ryszard Andruszkiewicz, a visiting scholar from the Technical University of Gdansk, to synthesize a series of 3-alkyl-GABA and 3-alkyl-glutamate analogs. He then wanted to investigate their inhibition of GABA aminotransferase and glutamate decarboxylase (GAD) [in 1988].

The S-isomer of 3-isobutyl-GABA, which would become “pregabalin,” had turned out to be the best anticonvulsant in the series and one of the best Parke-Davis had ever tested. A license agreement was signed between Warner-Lambert (parent of Parke-Davis) and Northwestern University [in 1990]. A patent was applied for.

Pfizer purchased Warner-Lambert [in 2000], giving it the exclusive rights to continue developing pregabalin.

Pregabalin, under the name Lyrica, entered the European market in September 2004. The FDA approved it December 2004, enabling pregabalin (Lyrica) to enter the US market in September 2005. At the time, it was the first medication approved for both diabetic peripheral neuropathy and postherpetic neuralgia.

Northwestern initially received a royalty of 6% of net sales from pregabalin and a portion of that was shared with Silverman and Andruszkiewicz. During its first full year of sales, pregabalin obtained “blockbuster drug” status with $1.2 billion in global sales. Because the money was invested in the endowment and more money came in, the amount grew to around $1.4 billion, making it a large contributor to Northwestern’s endowment.

European regulators approved it as a treatment for generalized anxiety disorder [in 2006]. The FDA approved it for the treatment of fibromyalgia [in 2007].

## Source: Wikipedia

Pregabalin was first synthesized in 1990 by Richard Bruce Silverman at Northwestern University in Evanston, Illinois. Lyrica was approved for medical use in the European Union in 2004 and in the United States in 2005. It was the first drug approved by the FDA for the treatment of fibromyalgia. In 2019, the United Kingdom reclassified pregabalin as a Class C controlled substance.
