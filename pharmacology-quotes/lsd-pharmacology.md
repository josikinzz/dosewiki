## Source: Disregard Everything I Say

The mechanism that produces the psychedelic effects of LSD is a direct result from its action as a 5-HT2A serotonin serotonin receptor agonist in the brain, a mechanism of action shared by all other hallucinogenic tryptamines and phenethylamines.

Various studies have shown that in reasonable doses in a careful context, it presents no negative cognitive, psychiatric, or toxic physical consequences of any sort. The median lethal dose or dosage at which 50% of participants die (LD50) of LSD for human beings has never been reached in any setting and is predicted to be roughly 12,000 micrograms, based on studies involving rats whereas the active dosage is between 100 and 500 micrograms.

## Source: DrugBank

The mechanism of action of LSD is not fully understood. It is thought to act as an agonist at the 5-HT2A receptor, and also as an agonist at the 5-HT1A, 5-HT2B, and 5-HT6 receptors.

| Target | Actions | Organism |

| 5-hydroxytryptamine receptor 2A | inhibitor | Humans |
| 5-hydroxytryptamine receptor 2B | agonist | Humans |
| 5-hydroxytryptamine receptor 6 | Not Available | Humans |

Rapidly absorbed.

Hepatic metabolism.

Half-life: 3 hours.

Estimates for the lethal dosage (LD50) of LSD range from 200 µg/kg to more than 1 mg/kg of human body mass, though most sources report that there are no known human cases of such an overdose.

## Source: PsychonautWiki

LSD's psychedelic effects are believed to come from its efficacy as a partial agonist at the 5-HT2A receptor. However, the exact role of these interactions and how they result in the psychedelic experience continues to remain elusive.

LSD is a non-selective 5-HT receptor agonist. It also has been found to bind to most dopamine receptor subtypes as well as all adrenoreceptor subtypes.

LSD is thought to have a relatively high affinity for 5-HT2A receptors compared to other psychedelics.

LSD is hepatically metabolized by CYP450 isoenzymes. The main metabolite is 2-oxo-3-hydroxy-LSD.

## Source: Wikipedia

LSD's effects on the central nervous system are dose-dependent.

LSD is a very potent psychedelic substance and its unique properties include its long duration of action, its complex metabolic pathway, and its high affinity for the 5-HT2A receptor.

LSD is an agonist of the 5-HT2A, 5-HT2B, 5-HT2C, 5-HT1A, 5-HT1B, 5-HT1D, 5-HT1E, 5-HT1F, 5-HT5A, 5-HT6, and 5-HT7 receptors. It is also an agonist of the D1, D2, D3, D4, and D5 dopamine receptors.

LSD is metabolized in the liver by the CYP450 isoenzymes. The major metabolite is 2-oxo-3-hydroxy-LSD.
