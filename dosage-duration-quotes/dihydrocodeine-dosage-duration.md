## Source: DrugBank

Dihydrocodeine is an opioid analgesic used as an alternative or adjunct to codeine to treat moderate to severe pain, severe dyspnea, and cough.

### Indication
In heroin addicts, dihydrocodeine has been used as a substitute drug, in doses up to 2500mg/day to treat addiction.

### Absorption
Bioavailability is low (approximately 20%) if administered orally.

Time to peak values are 1.6 and 1.8hours for a 30mg and 60mg dose, respectively.

## Source: Wikipedia

It was first described in 1911 and approved for medical use in 1948.

### Medical uses
For use against pain, dihydrocodeine is usually formulated as tablets or capsules containing 15–16 mg or 30–32 mg... Controlled release dihydrocodeine is available for both pain and coughing... as waxy tablets containing 60 to 120 mg of the drug.

### Pharmacology
30 mg of subcutaneous dihydrocodeine is equianalgesic to 10 mg of morphine. Another source states that dihydrocodeine is twice as strong as codeine...

After oral absorption, the drug is absorbed relatively rapidly with mean peak concentration at 1.7 hours. The mean half-life is 4 hours. The mean bioavailability of orally administered drug is 21%.

### Recreational use
The typical recreational dose can be anything from 70 mg to 500 mg, or, in users with tolerance, even more.
