### Pharmacology

Although 3-FEA has not been formally studied on the same level as traditional [amphetamines](https://psychonautwiki.org/wiki/Amphetamines) , it is currently assumed that like other [substituted amphetamines](https://psychonautwiki.org/wiki/Substituted_amphetamines) with substitutions at similar positions, it most likely acts primarily as a triple [reuptake inhibitor](https://psychonautwiki.org/wiki/Reuptake_inhibitor) and/or [releaser](https://psychonautwiki.org/wiki/Releaser) of the [monoamine](https://psychonautwiki.org/wiki/Monoamine) [neurotransmitters](https://psychonautwiki.org/wiki/Neurotransmitters) [serotonin](https://psychonautwiki.org/wiki/Serotonin) , [dopamine](https://psychonautwiki.org/wiki/Dopamine) , and [norepinephrine](https://psychonautwiki.org/wiki/Norepinephrine) .

It has been demonstrated that compared to the unsubstituted ethylamphetamine, 3-fluoroethamphetamine is a weaker releaser of [dopamine](https://psychonautwiki.org/wiki/Dopamine) , but a stronger releaser of both [serotonin](https://psychonautwiki.org/wiki/Serotonin) and [norepinephrine](https://psychonautwiki.org/wiki/Norepinephrine) , producing the strongest reinforcing effects in animal studies out of a range of 3-substituted amphetamine derivatives tested, despite not being the most potent dopamine releaser.

This indicates that 3-FEA effectively increases the levels of all the three major [monoamine neurotransmitters](https://psychonautwiki.org/wiki/Monoamine_neurotransmitters) dopamine, norepinephrine, and serotonin in the brain by acting as a releasing agent of said neurotransmitters and/or by binding to and partially [blocking the transporter proteins](https://psychonautwiki.org/wiki/Reuptake_inhibitor) that normally clear those molecules from the [synaptic cleft](https://psychonautwiki.org/wiki/Synaptic_cleft) after they have fulfilled their function of conducting a neural impulse. This transporter blockade allows these molecules to accumulate within core synaptic regions of the brain to extra-endogenous levels, resulting in a combination of [relaxing](https://psychonautwiki.org/wiki/Muscle_relaxation) , [stimulating](https://psychonautwiki.org/wiki/Stimulating) , [disinhibiting](https://psychonautwiki.org/wiki/Disinhibition) and [euphoric](https://psychonautwiki.org/wiki/Euphoria) effects associated with [entactogenic](https://psychonautwiki.org/wiki/Entactogenic) [substituted amphetamines](https://psychonautwiki.org/wiki/Substituted_amphetamines) such as [MDMA](https://psychonautwiki.org/wiki/MDMA) or [4-FA](https://psychonautwiki.org/wiki/4-FA) . [*[citation needed](https://psychonautwiki.org/wiki/Citation_needed)*]

### Toxicity and harm potential

Due to its [serotonin](https://psychonautwiki.org/wiki/Serotonin) -releasing [entactogenic](https://psychonautwiki.org/wiki/Entactogenic) properties, it is possible 3-FEA may display significant affinity and activity at the 5-HT 2B receptor, which like 5-HT 2B agonists such as [MDMA](https://psychonautwiki.org/wiki/MDMA) and fenfluramine would make it cardiotoxic with long-term or heavy use.

## Source: Wikipedia

3-Fluoroethamphetamine (3-FEA) is a stimulant drug of the amphetamine class which acts as a releasing agent of the monoamine neurotransmitters norepinephrine, dopamine and serotonin.
Compared to the unsubstituted ethylamphetamine, 3-fluoroethamphetamine is a weaker releaser of noradrenaline, but a stronger releaser of both dopamine and serotonin, and produced the strongest reinforcing effects in animal studies out of a range of 3-substituted ethamphetamine derivatives tested, despite not being the most potent dopamine releaser.
