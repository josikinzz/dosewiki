## Source: The Drug Classroom

Muslims are some of the most avid users of khat, yet there are ongoing debates in some countries over the permissibility of the drug. Some argue it’s prohibited under Islamic law. Others claim it’s endorsed as a prayer aid and has been used since the time of Muhammad.

#### 1935
Two reports on the issue of khat usage were released by the Advisory Committee of the League of Nations on the Traffic of Dangerous Drugs.

#### 1971
The UN Commission on Narcotic Drugs directed the UN Narcotics Laboratory to reinvestigate the chemical composition of khat. Multiple studies came out of that order, eventually leading to the isolation of S-(-)-a-aminopropiophenone from khat leaves.

#### 1993
The DEA classified cathinone as a Schedule 1 substance.

#### 2006
The DEA executed Operation Somali Express. It was the culmination of an 18-month investigation that led to the takedown of a 44-member trafficking group responsible for bringing 25 tons of khat from the Horn of Africa to the US.

**Khat use**
Khat remains very common in parts of the Middle East and Africa. It’s used in Somalia, Yemen, Ethiopia, and Kenya, among other locations.
[...]
In Somalia, 20 tons of khat worth $800,000 were imported daily from Kenya prior to a ban from the Supreme Islamic Courts Council. After the ban went away, the trade of khat just in Hargeisa, Somalia was reported to be worth $300,000 per day.

**Expansion to new regions**
Khat has expanded into the West due to its use by immigrant communities, such as Somalis in upstate New York and the UK. Though there’s now a lot of evidence supporting its use in the West, it hasn’t really been used outside of immigrant populations.
