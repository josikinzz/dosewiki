### Pharmacodynamics

It has a duration of action of several hours and a wider therapeutic index as patients develop tolerance to opioids.

### Absorption

Fentanyl sublingual tablets are 54% bioavailable, transmucosal lozenges are 50% bioavailable, buccal tablets are 65% bioavailable, sublingual spray is 76% bioavailable, and nasal spray is approximately 64% bioavailable.

Fentanyl transmucosal lozenges reach a Cmax ... with a Tmax of 20-40 minutes.
Fentanyl sublingual spray reached a Cmax ... with a Tmax of 0.69-1.25 hours, decreasing as the dose increased.
Fentanyl transdermal systems reached a Cmax ... with a Tmax of 3.6±1.3h for a 25µg/h dose.
Fentanyl nasal spray reaches a Cmax ... with a Tmax of less than 1 hour for a 200µg/100µL dose.

Fentanyl is ... approximately 40 to 50 times more potent than pharmaceutical grade (i.e. 100% pure) heroin and roughly 80 to 100 times more potent than morphine.

## Source: Wikipedia

Intravenous fentanyl is often used for anesthesia... These are often given in 15–30 minute intervals throughout procedures...

Fentanyl is the most commonly used intrathecal opioid because its lipophilic profile allows a quick onset of action (5–10 min) and intermediate duration of action (60–120 min).

Intranasal fentanyl is available in doses of 50, 100, 200, 400(PecFent) μg.

The patches work by slowly releasing fentanyl through the skin into the bloodstream over 48 to 72 hours... Each patch should be changed every 72 hours. ... Under normal circumstances, the patch will reach its full effect within 12 to 24 hours...
