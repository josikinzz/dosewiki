## Source: PsychonautWiki

**Do not use 4-FMA if you have a history of heart-related issues or experience severe headache after its use.** We have been made aware of a report released by Trimbos-instituut and Nationaal Vergiftigingen Informatie Centrum (NVIC), describing incidents of strokes after an increased use of the closely related analogue 4-FA, and there is no reason this does not apply to 4-FMA as well. In addition to the common amphetamine-like effects (agitation, anxiety, tachycardia, hypertension, chest pain et al.), serious cardio- and cerebrovascular complications have been reported, including rhythm (sinus arrhythmia, ventricular extrasystoles (bigeminy), conduction disturbances) and acute cardiac failure.

Very little is known about the pharmacological properties, metabolism, and toxicity of 4-FMA. 4-FMA use may produce dependence and abuse as well as damage to the brain and other organs.

It has been reported to be more likely to produce side effects like headaches and cardiovascular effects than similar substances.

Some reports suggest it tends to come with more side effects and bodily strain than other fluorinated amphetamines, explaining its lack of popularity and availability.

Such usage would be strongly discouraged however, due to the increased risks for neurotoxicity and other dangerous side-effects.

## Toxicity and harm potential

The toxicity and long-term health effects of recreational 4-FMA use has not been studied in any scientific context and the exact toxic dosage is unknown.

4-FMA is reported to be particularly caustic in comparison to other compounds and can, therefore, cause chemical burns within the nasal passage and throat if it is insufflated.

### Tolerance and addiction potential

As with other stimulants, the chronic use of 4-FMA can be considered moderately addictive with a high potential for abuse and is capable of causing psychological dependence among certain users.

### Psychosis

Abuse of compounds within the amphetamine chemical class at high dosages for prolonged periods of time can potentially result in a stimulant psychosis that may present with a variety of symptoms (e.g., paranoia, hallucinations, or delusions). A review on treatment for amphetamine, dextroamphetamine, and methamphetamine abuse-induced psychosis states that about 5–15% of users fail to recover completely.

### Dangerous interactions

- **Alcohol** - risk of excessive drinking and liver damage
- **GHB/GBL** - respiratory arrest risk
- **Opioids** - respiratory arrest risk
- **Cocaine** - cardiac effects and serotonin-related valvulopathy
- **Cannabis** - increased anxiety, thought loops and paranoia
- **Caffeine** - increased strain on the heart
- **Tramadol** - increased seizure risk
- **DXM** - heart issues
- **Ketamine** - psychoses resembling schizophrenia
- **PCP** - tachycardia, hypertension, manic states
- **25x-NBOMe** - tachycardia, hypertension, vasoconstriction, heart failure, seizures
- **MAOIs** - hypertensive crises
