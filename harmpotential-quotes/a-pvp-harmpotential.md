### Hospitalizations and Deaths

**It has been linked to numerous hospitalizations and overdose deaths.**

### Addiction and Compulsive Behavior

Like other synthetic cathinones, **α-PVP is associated with compulsive use and addiction**.

### Unknown Toxicity

**Very little data exists about the pharmacological properties, metabolism, and toxicity of a-PVP.** Due to its potent psychostimulant effects and unknown toxicity profile, it is **highly advised to use harm reduction practices** if using with this substance.

### Cardiac Effects

**Abnormal heartbeat** - Due to the potency of its rush, α-PVP can cause **various uncomfortable or painful sensations in the heart, especially when abused** or used for extended periods. **Those with genetic a family history of heart issues are discouraged from using this drug** in its most potent methods of administration, if not avoid it entirely.

**Increased blood pressure** - α-PVP, especially when it is vaporized or injected, can lead to **sudden spikes of blood pressure that may manifest in an extremely uncomfortable "exploding heart" sensation**.

### Seizure Risk

**Seizure - α-PVP may lower the seizure threshold in some individuals**, especially when it is abused.

### Extreme Paranoia

**α-PVP can lead to states of extreme paranoia when abused or dosed too highly**, which is why eyeballing this substance is strongly discouraged.

### Psychosis

**Psychosis - This compound is known to readily induce psychosis if it is abused for prolonged periods of time.**

α-PVP, like other stimulants, **can result in a stimulant psychosis that may present with a variety of symptoms** (e.g., paranoia, hallucinations, or delusions). A review on treatment for amphetamine, dextroamphetamine, and methamphetamine abuse-induced psychosis states that **about 5–15% of users fail to recover completely**.

### Deaths

**α-PVP has been reported to be the cause, or a significant contributory cause, of death in suicides and overdoses caused by combinations of drugs.** **α-PVP has also been linked to at least one death where it was combined with pentedrone and caused heart failure.**

### Dependence and Abuse Potential

The **chronic use of α-PVP can be considered highly addictive with a high potential for abuse** and is capable of causing **psychological dependence** among certain users. When addiction has developed, **cravings and withdrawal effects may occur** if a person suddenly stops their usage.

### Dangerous Interactions

**Warning:** *Many psychoactive substances that are reasonably safe to use on their own can suddenly become **dangerous and even life-threatening** when combined with certain other substances.*

- **25x-NBOMe & 25x-NBOH** - Risk of **seizures, and heart failure in extreme cases**.
- **Alcohol** - Can result in blackouts and **severe respiratory depression**.
- **DXM** - Increased risk of panic attacks and hypertensive crisis, or **serotonin syndrome**.
- **Stimulants** - May **increase one's heart rate and blood pressure to dangerous levels**.

## Source: Wikipedia

### Adverse Effects

**α-PVP, like other stimulants, can cause hyperstimulation, paranoia, and hallucinations.** α-PVP has been reported to be the **cause, or a significant contributory cause of death in suicides and overdoses** caused by combinations of drugs. α-PVP has also been **linked to at least one death with pulmonary edema and moderately advanced atherosclerotic coronary artery disease** when it was combined with pentedrone.

According to Craig Crespi in the journal Case Reports in Psychiatry, **"symptoms are known to easily escalate into frightening delusions, paranoid psychosis, extreme agitation, and a multitude of other altered mental states."**

### Emergency Room Effects

In emergency settings, commonly observed effects have included **psychosis-like state, agitation, aggression, hallucinations, abnormal heart rate, and vomiting**.

### Recreational Effects Include Danger Signs

The effects of α-PVP include cocaine-like psychostimulant effects, euphoria, elevated mood, alertness, pleasurable rush, feeling "sped up", mania-like symptoms... **insomnia, paranoia, hallucinations, and increased aggression and violence**.

### Australian Deaths

The drug was explicitly made illegal in New South Wales after it was illegally marketed... **The legislative action followed the death of two individuals from using it; one jumping off a balcony, another having a heart attack after a state of delirium.**

### Potency Warning

**It is one of the most potent dopamine reuptake inhibitors in vitro known.** The drug is much more potent than amphetamine as an NDRI in in vitro, with **46-fold greater potency** in terms of dopamine reuptake inhibition.
