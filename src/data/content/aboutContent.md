**dose.wiki** compiles knowledge about psychoactive drugs into one open dataset, readable here or as a download. It holds four collections: **{{compoundCount}}** [substance articles](/substances), **{{effectCount}}** [subjective effect articles](/effects), **{{replicationCount}}** [replications](/replications), and **{{reportCount}}** [trip reports](/reports).

A substance article covers dose, duration, effects, interactions, chemistry, and legality by country. An effect article describes one component of an altered state, from visual drifting to ego death. A replication is an image, video, or sound that reproduces an effect, made by an artist who has experienced it. A trip report is one person's account of one experience.

Everything a substance article needs is already published, spread across ten reference sites in ten formats. Compiling it was always the expensive part. We wrote hundreds of substance articles by hand for earlier reference sites, so we know the cost: each article is days of work, each writer sets a different standard, and when a writer leaves, the method leaves with them.

Compiling is now what software does well, so substance articles begin as pipeline drafts. Code copies the dose and duration tables. An LLM reads the sources and drafts the narrative sections from word-for-word excerpts, citing the source of each one. Human editors take over from there. They verify the pharmacology, check the doses and interactions where a wrong figure can hurt someone, and add what the sources left out. Each article's status subsection shows how much human review it has had, and [How articles are made](/docs/how) shows every step and every prompt.

Effect articles, replications, and trip reports are made by people, not pipelines. The effect articles are the Subjective Effect Index, written by contributors over more than a decade across several websites, and dose.wiki is its current home. Replications are made by artists trying to show what an experience looked like from the inside, most of them from the [r/replications](https://reddit.com/r/replications) community on Reddit.

Everything we write is dedicated to the public domain under [CC0](/docs/license), including the articles and the schema behind the dataset. The site's code is MIT. Trip reports, replications, the TripSit interaction data, and reagent test data keep their own terms. You can download the datasets on the [Open Data](#data) tab, use them in your own project, or build another encyclopedia on top of them. We want that to happen.

dose.wiki is in **beta**. The database is live and growing, coverage is incomplete, and most substance articles are still pipeline drafts awaiting human review. Structure and features will keep changing on the way to 1.0. If something looks wrong or missing, tell us through the [feedback form](/about/feedback) or at <contact@dose.wiki>.
