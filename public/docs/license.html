How you can use dose.wiki

licensing & reuse — the code, the content, and the two parts we don’t own

dose.wiki is built to be taken. Almost everything here — every article we wrote and the entire structured dataset behind them — is released into the public domain: use it, remix it, sell it, build on it, with no permission and no credit required. The website’s source code is MIT. The exceptions are the community trip reports, which belong to the people who wrote them, and two sections we don’t own — interaction ratings from TripSit and reagent test results from protestkit.eu. This page lays out exactly what you can do with each part.

02 Why dose.wiki is this open
dose.wiki exists to be a modern, open, comprehensive harm-reduction resource — and a dataset so easy to download, build on, and fork that nobody ever has to research and rebuild an encyclopedia of this kind from scratch again.

That goal only works if the license gets out of the way. An attribution or share-alike requirement is a small tax, but it is still a tax: it makes people stop, check, and worry about whether they are allowed. We would rather remove the question entirely. So the content and the dataset are dedicated to the public domain under CC0 — the most permissive release there is. No attribution, no share-alike, no permission, no strings.

If dose.wiki vanished tomorrow, the dataset would already be in a thousand other hands, free for anyone to carry forward. That is the point.

03 The content — public domain (CC0)

Every article dose.wiki wrote, and the entire structured dataset, is released under CC0 1.0 Universal. In plain terms, we have waived our copyright and placed the work into the public domain. You can do essentially anything with it:

You may
  • Copy & redistribute the text and data anywhere, in any format.
  • Remix, adapt, translate and build on it however you like.
  • Use it commercially — paid apps, books, products, research.
  • Skip attribution — no credit line is required.
  • Skip permission — you never need to ask us.
  • Relicense your version — there is no share-alike obligation.
What this covers
  • All written article sections we authored — summary, pharmacology, tolerance, harm potential, history & culture, legality, and the dosing/duration notes.
  • The subjective effects content, built on the documentation work of Josie Kins.
  • The full structured dataset and its schema — SubstanceIndex.json.
Credit is appreciated, never required. CC0 asks nothing of you. But if dose.wiki is useful, a link back to dose.wiki or a mention of Josie Kins genuinely helps the project keep going — it is a courtesy, not an obligation.
What sits outside this. The community trip reports belong to the people who wrote them (§5), and the interaction and reagent data are not ours to dedicate (§6). Everything else — our writing and the dataset — is CC0.
04 The code — MIT

dose.wiki is open-source software as well as open content, and the two are licensed separately. The website, the data tooling, and the build that runs the public site are released under the MIT License — short, permissive, and familiar to any developer.

You may
  • Use the code in your own projects — open or closed source.
  • Modify, merge, publish, distribute, sublicense, or sell it.
  • Use it commercially, with no royalty.
The one condition

Keep the MIT copyright and permission notice in copies or substantial portions of the code. That is the entire obligation. The software is provided “as is,” without warranty.

Code vs. content. MIT covers the software; the content and data are CC0 (§3). If you fork the repository you are taking both — the code under MIT, the data under CC0.
05 Trip reports

Trip reports are different from everything above. They are personal, first-person accounts written by other people — some donated by friends years ago “to do whatever with,” others emailed in by strangers, and more recently submitted through the report form. They are creative works, and copyright stays with the author who wrote each one. We publish them with permission; we don’t claim to own them, so they are not part of the CC0 dedication of our own content and not included in the downloadable dataset. How a report may be reused depends on how it reached us — and every report on the site carries a small marker saying which case it falls under:

Already on the site

Up to the author

The legacy archive — donated by friends or emailed in by strangers, often years ago. Rights stay with the original author. Read and share them freely with credit to the author, but reuse beyond that — especially anything commercial — is up to that author, so check with them first. If a report is yours and you want it credited differently, relicensed, or removed, just ask — we’ll honor it.

New, via the submission form

Public domain (CC0)

Reports submitted through the report form are dedicated to the public domain under CC0 by the person who submits them: anyone may reuse them, including commercially, with no attribution required — the same terms as the rest of our own content. The submission form makes this dedication explicit before a report is sent.

Why the difference? The older reports were given to us informally, long before any license existed, and we can’t reach most of those authors to confirm broader terms — so we leave the rights with them and just ask reusers to credit them. New submissions come in through a form that asks the submitter to release the report into the public domain, so those can be fully free. Either way, a contributor can always ask us to change or remove their report.
06 What we don’t own

Two sections on every article come straight from other projects. dose.wiki did not write them and does not own them, so we cannot place them in the public domain. They keep the terms their creators set. These are the only carve-outs from the CC0 dedication.

Interactions — TripSit

The combination risk ratings shown on every article come from TripSit’s drug combination research. TripSit publishes this data under its own terms, not a standard open license:

“Use of the data the combination chart and app are built upon is free-of-charge for non-commercial purposes… we only require that you link back to either this page, or combo.tripsit.me. This should be accompanied with a note citing TripSit as the source for the information wherever it appears. The presentation should also include a note that the information is only intended for a quick overview and reference, and that it is necessary for users to perform more individual research before making a decision.”

— TripSit, “Drug combinations” (Use & Attribution)

If you reuse the interaction data, you must:

  • Credit TripSit and link back to their combination page or combo.tripsit.me.
  • Keep it non-commercial — commercial use, reselling printed charts, or editing the chart needs TripSit’s written permission.
  • Include the “quick reference, do your own research” disclaimer.

This applies whether you take the data from TripSit or from us — it is their data, cleared with them, not with dose.wiki.

Reagent tests — protestkit.eu

The reagent color reactions (Marquis, Mecke, Mandelin, Simon’s, Froehde, Liebermann, Ehrlich, and others) are fetched live from protestkit.eu when you open an article. Because they are pulled at view time, they are not part of the downloadable dataset, and they remain under ProtestKit’s own terms. To reuse reagent results, get them from — and clear them with — protestkit.eu directly, and credit protestkit.eu as the source.

The CC0 dedication does not extend to these two sections. Everything else on dose.wiki — all of our own writing and the entire dataset — is yours to use freely.

08 Why we can release it this way

dose.wiki’s pipeline starts from other people’s published pages, so it is fair to ask how we can turn around and place the result in the public domain. Here is the reasoning.

The published text is new writing

Source text exists only in our internal excerpt notes — working material, like a researcher’s quotations. The published prose is written fresh under prompts that forbid copying sentences, then edited by a human reviewer. The expression is ours.

Facts are not owned

That a substance was first made in a given year, that a dose range is what it is, that a law schedules it — these are facts, and nobody owns facts. Copyright protects expression, and ours is original, re-verified against citable references.

The dataset is our compilation

The schema, the normalization, and the selection and arrangement of every field into one consistent structure are dose.wiki’s own work. That compilation is ours to give away.

So we give away as much as we can

What we own — our writing and our dataset — we release under CC0. What belongs to others — the trip reports their authors wrote, and the TripSit and protestkit.eu sections — we leave under their own terms and credit honestly. No more, no less.
