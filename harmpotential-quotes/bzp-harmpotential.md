## Source: Wikipedia

### Acute Psychosis, Renal Toxicity, Seizures

**Adverse effects have been reported following its use including acute psychosis, renal toxicity and seizures.**

### Death from BZP Alone

**Deaths from piperazine derivatives are extremely rare, but there has been at least one death apparently due to BZP alone.**

### Amphetamine-Like with Abuse Liability

The study suggested that BZP "**should be placed under statutory control similar to those regulating the use of amphetamine**".

It was discovered that BZP had side effects and was largely abandoned as a worm treatment. It next appears in the literature in the 1970s when it was investigated as a potential antidepressant medication, but rejected when research reported that **BZP had amphetamine-like effects and was liable to abuse**.

### Sold as Fake MDMA

**BZP, which is often mixed with TFMPP, has been claimed to be a safer alternative to other illicit street drugs. It is also used as an adulterant of or substitute for MDMA**, which has similar effects, and **is sometimes marketed as "ecstasy"**, a colloquial term for MDMA.

### Toxic Effects and Life-Threatening Seizures

**Fourteen toxic seizures were recorded in two patients with life-threatening toxicity with status epilepticus and severe respiratory and metabolic acidosis.** The results of this study and others like it showed that **BZP can cause unpredictable and serious toxicity in some individuals**.

### Extensive Side Effects

The major side effects include **dilated pupils, blurred vision, dryness of the mouth, extreme alertness, pruritus, confusion, agitation, tremor, extrapyramidal symptoms (dystonia, akathisia), headache, dizziness, anxiety, insomnia, vomiting, chest pain, hallucinations, paresthesia, tachycardia, hypertension, palpitations, collapse, hyperventilation, sweating, hyperthermia and problems with urine retention**.

### Severe Toxic Effects

**The more severe toxic effects include psychosis or adverse psychiatric events, renal toxicity, respiratory failure, hyperthermia, serotonin syndrome, rhabdomyolysis and seizure.**

### Fatal Blood Concentration

**One death has been attributed to ingestion of BZP alone; in this case its blood concentration was measured to be 8 mg/L.**

### Multiple Deaths with Combinations

**Combined with alcohol or other illicit drugs, such as TFMPP and MDMA, multiple deaths have been reported.**

A combination of BZP and MDMA ingested by a 23-year-old DJ **nearly resulted in death. He was put into an induced coma**, and later recovered.

In another case in Zürich in 2001, **a 23-year-old who had taken BZP and MDMA died from a massive cerebral edema 57 hours after hospital admission**.

### Substitutes for Methamphetamine in Addicted Animals

Studies undertaken on animals have indicated that **BZP can substitute for methamphetamine in addicted rats**, although it is one-tenth as potent and produces correspondingly weaker addictive effects.

### Banned in Many Countries

**BZP is banned in Australia, Austria, Canada, Denmark, Estonia, France, Germany, Greece, Ireland, Italy, Japan, Malta, Poland, Sweden and the United States.**

### DEA Misclassification

The drug was federally classified as a Schedule I controlled substance in the United States in 2002, following a report by the DEA **which incorrectly stated that BZP was 10 to 20 times more potent than amphetamine, when in fact BZP is ten times less potent than dexamphetamine**.
