## Source: Wikipedia

### Psychoactivity Introduction

**"High doses can cause serious toxic effects including acute psychosis, with risks heightened during pregnancy and with psychiatric conditions."**

### Essential Oil / Myristicin Toxicity

**"In its pure form, myristicin is a toxin, and consumption of excessive amounts of nutmeg can result in myristicin poisoning."**

### Effects - Convulsions and Adverse Symptoms

**"Myristicin—a monoamine oxidase inhibitor and psychoactive substance—can cause convulsions, palpitations, nausea, eventual dehydration, and generalized body pain when consumed in large amounts."**

### Effects - Drug Interactions and Allergic Reactions

**"Nutmeg may interact with anxiolytic drugs, produce allergic reactions, cause contact dermatitis, and evoke acute episodes of psychosis."**

### Effects - Deliriant Intoxication Symptoms

**"Varying considerably from person to person, nutmeg intoxication may occur with side effects, such as delirium, anxiety, confusion, headaches, nausea, dizziness, dry mouth, eye irritation, and amnesia."**

### Effects - Duration and Fatality Note

**"Intoxication takes several hours to reach maximum effect, and may last for several days. Incidents of fatal poisoning from nutmeg and myristicin individually are uncommon."**

### Toxicity During Pregnancy

**"If consumed in large amounts, nutmeg could cause premature labor and miscarriage. Nutmeg may also interact with pain relievers such as pethidine, so avoiding it during pregnancy is recommended."**

### Toxicity to Pets

**"The scent of nutmeg may attract pets, but it can be poisonous if consumed in excess."**
