## Source: PsychonautWiki

**F-Phenibut** (also known as **4-Fluorophenibut** , **Fluorophenibut** , **Fluorobut** , **Baflofen** and **CGP-11130** ) is a central nervous system [depressant](https://psychonautwiki.org/wiki/Depressant) and closely related structural analog of [phenibut](https://psychonautwiki.org/wiki/Phenibut) . F-Phenibut possesses an effect profile similar to [phenibut](https://psychonautwiki.org/wiki/Phenibut) but with a faster [onset](https://psychonautwiki.org/wiki/Onset) , significantly increased potency, and shorter [total duration](https://psychonautwiki.org/wiki/Duration) . It has recently become available through online [research chemical](https://psychonautwiki.org/wiki/Research_chemical) vendors, although little is known about this substance, particularly [its potential toxicity](https://psychonautwiki.org/wiki/Research_chemicals#Toxicity_and_harm_potential) and addiction potential.

Early reports suggest that in comparison to other commonly used [GABAgenic](https://psychonautwiki.org/wiki/GABA) depressants such as [alcohol](https://psychonautwiki.org/wiki/Alcohol) or [benzodiazepines](https://psychonautwiki.org/wiki/Benzodiazepines) , this compound produces less inebriation and more euphoria, giving it more recreational potential as well as a correspondingly higher risk of abuse.

## Source: Wikipedia

4-Fluorophenibut (developmental code name CGP-11130; also known as β-(4-fluorophenyl)-γ-aminobutyric acid or β-(4-fluorophenyl)-GABA) is a GABAB receptor agonist which was never marketed. It is selective for the GABAB receptor over the GABAA receptor (IC50 = 1.70 μM and > 100 μM, respectively). The drug is a GABA analogue and is closely related to baclofen (β-(4-chlorophenyl)-GABA), tolibut (β-(4-methylphenyl)-GABA), and phenibut (β-phenyl-GABA). It is less potent as a GABAB receptor agonist than baclofen but more potent than phenibut.

## Legal status

F-Phenibut is a prohibited substance in Lithuania and Hungary.
