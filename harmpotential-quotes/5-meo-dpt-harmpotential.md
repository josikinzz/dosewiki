## Source: Wikipedia

### Shulgin's Assessment

According to Shulgin, **5-MeO-DPT's actions are ambiguous and not totally positive**. This led to him tucking discussion of the drug away in the 5-MeO-DET entry of TiHKAL as opposed to giving 5-MeO-DPT its own entry in the book.

### Negative Effects at Higher Doses

At the 8.4 mg dose, there were 5-MeO-DMT-like **"head noises" or "bells" described as "bad"** and an underlying 5-MeO-DMT-like "turn on" described as "good". However, per Shulgin, while these effects alternated, **the unpleasant negative effects overall outweighed the positive and desired effects**.

Shulgin stated that he had **"better things to do with my time"** and did not further explore 5-MeO-DPT or evaluate higher doses.

### Related Compound Side Effects

5-MeO-DPT's lower homologue 5-MeO-DET was found to produce **unique and strong side effects such as lightheadedness, dizziness, and vertigo at low doses which precluded it from being tolerated or used at hallucinogenic doses**.

Shulgin synthesized and tested 5-MeO-DPT in the hopes that the vertigo-related side effects of 5-MeO-DET would be reduced or eliminated. However, while 5-MeO-DET-like side effects were not described, **Shulgin nonetheless deemed 5-MeO-DPT an unpromising compound**.

### Legal Status

**United States** - 5-MeO-DPT is considered a **Schedule I controlled substance** as a positional isomer of 5-MeO-DiPT.
