### Extreme Potency

Studies have estimated acetylfentanyl to be between **five to fifteen times more potent than heroin, eighty times more potent than morphine**, and fifteen times less potent than its parent compound, fentanyl.

### Hundreds of Deaths

**Fentanyl analogs have killed hundreds of people throughout Europe and the former Soviet republics** since the most recent resurgence in use began in Estonia in the early 2000s.

### US Fatalities

**The United States Drug Enforcement Administration reported in July of 2015 that at least 52 confirmed fatalities involving acetylfentanyl in the United States had occurred between 2013-2015.** **Ten fatalities attributed to acetylfentanyl overdose were reported during March of 2013 alone in Rhode Island.**

### Respiratory Depression

The side effects of fentanyl analogs are similar to those of fentanyl itself, which include itchiness, nausea and **potentially fatal respiratory depression**.

### Sold as Heroin

On the street market, **fentanyl and its analogs are often misrepresented as traditional opioids like heroin** due to its increased potency and substantially lower production costs.

### Skin Absorption Danger

Particular care must be taken with acetylfentanyl due to its extreme potency and **ability to be absorbed through the skin. This means that simply unintentionally spilling a very small amount of acetylfentanyl on one's skin could result in a fatal overdose.**

### Overdose Deaths from Vomit Aspiration

Acetylfentanyl can also cause nausea and vomiting; **a significant number of deaths attributed to opioid overdose are caused by aspiration of vomit by an unconscious victim**.

### Extreme Addiction Potential

The chronic use of acetylfentanyl can be considered **extremely addictive with a high potential for abuse** and is capable of causing **psychological dependence** among certain users.

### Dangerous Interactions

- **Alcohol** - Both substances potentiate sedation, can lead to unexpected loss of consciousness. **Memory blackouts are likely**
- **Stimulants** - If the stimulant wears off first then **the opiate may overcome the user and cause respiratory arrest**
- **Benzodiazepines** - **Very rapidly leading to unconsciousness**. Vomit aspiration risk. **Blackouts/memory loss likely**
- **DXM** - **CNS depression, difficulty breathing, heart issues, and liver toxicity** have been observed
- **GHB/GBL** - **Very rapidly leading to unconsciousness**
- **MAOIs** - **Death has occurred in some cases**

## Source: Wikipedia

### Deaths Summary

**Fentanyl analogs have killed hundreds of people throughout Europe and the former Soviet republics** since the most recent resurgence in use began in Estonia in the early 2000s, and novel derivatives continue to appear.

### European Deaths

**Acetylfentanyl has been analytically confirmed in 32 fatalities in four European member states between 2013 and August 2015**, Germany (2), Poland (1), Sweden (27), and the United Kingdom (2).

### Rhode Island Outbreak

The Centers for Disease Control and Prevention (CDC) issued a health alert to report that between March 2013 and May 2013, **14 overdose deaths related to injected acetylfentanyl had occurred among intravenous drug users** (ages between 19 and 57 years) in Rhode Island.

### Pennsylvania Deaths

Pennsylvania confirmed at least one acetylfentanyl overdose death and **attributed at least 50 fatalities to either fentanyl or acetylfentanyl during the first half of 2013**.

### Japan Death

One fatal poisoning caused by intravenous injection of a "bath salt" product containing acetylfentanyl mixed with 4'-Methoxy-α-pyrrolidinopentiophenone has been reported in 2016.

### Overdose Treatment

**Larger than normal doses of the antidote may be required** [compared to heroin overdose].
