**dose.wiki** compiles knowledge about psychoactive substances into an open dataset. You can browse its four collections here or [download the datasets](#data) for reuse.

- **[{{compoundCount}} substance articles](/substances):** dose, duration, effects, interactions, chemistry, and legality by country.
- **[{{effectCount}} subjective effect articles](/effects):** descriptions of individual components of altered states, from [visual drifting](/effects/drifting) to [ego death](/effects/ego-death).
- **[{{replicationCount}} replications](/replications):** images, videos, and audio that recreate subjective effects.
- **[{{reportCount}} trip reports](/reports):** first-person accounts of individual experiences.

## Sources and review

Much of the information a substance article needs has already been gathered and published: decades of documentation by scientists, psychonauts, and the harm-reduction community. We draw on [ten harm-reduction and reference sites](/docs/how#sources), each with its own format, with no shared standard and no one site covering all of the information.

The shared structure makes information from different sources easier to compare, and makes gaps and disagreements easier to see. Editors add corrections, references, and explanations of their own. Each article links back to the source pages that informed it.

Substance articles begin with AI-written drafts and dose and duration tables transcribed from source sites by software. Human review involves verifying the pharmacology, checking dose and interaction information for errors that could cause harm, and adding information omitted by the sources.

The extent of human review varies by article. [Automated citation checks](/docs/how#citations) assess whether sources support claims; they do not establish that a human has reviewed an article. Each article's Status section records its human review status. [How articles are made](/docs/how) describes the process and includes the prompts used to prepare article drafts.

The other three collections are made by people. The effect articles are the [Subjective Effect Index](/effects), written over more than a decade across several websites. Replications are made by artists attempting to depict the subjective appearance of an experience, most of whom are from the [r/replications](https://reddit.com/r/replications) community on Reddit. Trip reports are written by their authors and reviewed by an editor before they are published.

dose.wiki is in **beta**. Coverage is incomplete, and most substance articles are still drafts awaiting human review. Structure and features will keep changing on the way to 1.0. If you notice a possible error or omission, [send us feedback](#contact).

## Project history

dose.wiki was co-founded by [Josie Kins](/contributors/josie) and [Lyrea](/contributors/lyrea). Josie created the Subjective Effect Index; Lyrea leads pharmacology and UX.

Josie began the Subjective Effect Index on her blog, Disregard Everything I Say, in 2011. She later founded [PsychonautWiki](https://psychonautwiki.rip), which launched in 2013, and continued developing the index through [Effect Index](https://effectindex.com), launched in 2017. dose.wiki is its current home.

The early articles were compiled by hand, with each article taking days of work. Contributors used different approaches without a shared, documented method. That experience informed dose.wiki's approach to bringing source material into a common structure.
