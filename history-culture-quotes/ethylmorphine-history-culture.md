## Source: PsychonautWiki

**Ethylmorphine** , also known as codethyline and dionine, is a semi-synthetic [morphinan](https://psychonautwiki.org/wiki/Morphinan) [opioid](https://psychonautwiki.org/wiki/Opioid) first created by Merck in 1884 and used as a weaker alternative to [morphine](https://psychonautwiki.org/wiki/Morphine) . Today, it is most commonly used as an [antitussive](https://psychonautwiki.org/wiki/Antitussive) which is available in cough syrup preparations like Cocillana, Cosylan, Solvipect comp. (in combination with guaifenesin) and Lepheton (in combination with [ephedrine](https://psychonautwiki.org/wiki/Ephedrine) ), or in tablet form sold as Tussipax (in combination with [codeine](https://psychonautwiki.org/wiki/Codeine) ). Cough syrup preparations commonly include a mild laxative to counter the [constipation](https://psychonautwiki.org/wiki/Constipation) which is a common side effect of opioids.

Ethylmorphine is also used in ophthalmic preparations as dionine to treat inflammations of the eye. In 1904, an ethylmorphine preparation was described as "a new ocular analgesic."

Internationally, Ethylmorphine is listed in the Single Convention on Narcotic Drugs in Schedule III.

## Source: Wikipedia

Ethylmorphine was first marketed in France in 1953 by Houde, and in Norway and Spain in 1960. It is not marketed in the United States and is a Schedule II controlled substance.
